Before you touch anything

Safety first

Most beginner damage comes from static electricity and from working on a unit that is still connected to power. Follow these steps every time.

Never skip

Shut down, unplug the power cable from the wall or unit, then hold the power button for about 5 seconds to drain leftover charge. Only then open the case.

  1. Power down and unplug. Switch off the PSU switch if there is one, and remove the power cable.
  2. Control static (ESD). Wear an anti-static wrist strap clipped to bare metal on the chassis. Without one, touch bare metal on the case before handling parts.
  3. Hold parts by their edges. Never touch chips, gold contacts or circuit traces. Keep parts in anti-static bags until needed.
  4. Work on a clear, dry, non-carpeted surface. Keep liquids away and keep screws in a small tray.
  5. Do not open the power supply. It can hold a dangerous charge even when unplugged. Replace the whole PSU instead.
  6. Photograph cable positions before removing them. It makes reassembly much easier.

Opening and inspecting a unit

  1. Place the case on its side with the removable panel facing up.
  2. Remove the thumbscrews or screws on the rear edge and slide the panel back.
  3. Look first. Check for dust, loose cables, bulging capacitors, burn marks or a fan that is not spinning.
  4. Note anything unusual before you change anything.

POST error indicators

Beep codes

When the computer runs its power-on self-test (POST), the motherboard speaker can signal a fault with beeps. Always check the motherboard manual, because codes differ by BIOS maker.

PatternCommon meaningFirst thing to try
1 shortPOST passed, normal startupNo action needed.
Continuous or repeating longMemory problem or power faultReseat RAM; test with one module.
1 long, 2 shortVideo / graphics fault (typical on AMI/Award)Reseat the graphics card and video cable.
1 long, 3 shortMemory or video memory error (varies)Reseat RAM, then the graphics card.
Repeating shortPower supply or motherboard faultCheck power connectors and PSU.
No beep, no displayNo speaker, dead board, or no powerCheck power first, then the speaker header.

Indicator LEDs and two-digit debug displays on some boards give the same information. Look up their meaning in the manual.

Tools and diagnostics

Tools you will use

Phillips screwdriver

Case panels, drive bays, most board screws. A magnetic tip helps.

Anti-static wrist strap

Equalizes charge between you and the chassis to protect components.

Compressed air or soft brush

Remove dust from fans, heatsinks and vents. Hold the fan blades still while you blow.

Multimeter or PSU tester

Checks whether the power supply is delivering correct voltages.

POST card / speaker

Shows or sounds the error code during startup.

Diagnostic software

Memory test utilities, drive health (SMART) viewers and temperature monitors help confirm a suspected fault.

Preventive maintenance

Keep it from breaking

Clean regularly

Dust blocks airflow. Clean vents, filters and fans every few months in dusty rooms.

Manage cables

Tie cables away from fans and keep air paths clear.

Keep it cool

Leave space around the case. Never block intake or exhaust vents.

Check connections

Reseat cables and modules that have been moved or transported.

Protect from power problems

Use a surge protector or UPS. Avoid cutting power during startup or shutdown.

Back up data

A drive failure is easier to handle when your files are copied elsewhere.
